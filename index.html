part1_story_hook.exe▫▫✕

What if the world you know was once someone else’s future?

In 2050, as a family prepares to leave an old-style Filipino home affected by government redevelopment, a teenager discovers an old CRT monitor in their parent’s former room. Among the belongings left behind are objects connected to Lola’s youth and a past the teenager has never experienced firsthand. When the monitor opens an interactive digital museum of Filipino popular culture from 2000 to 2010, the teenager steps into a world shaped by different technologies, cultural practices, and social circumstances.

Through Lola’s memories, the teenager explores how Filipinos communicated, entertained themselves, expressed their identities, and adapted to a changing society. What first appears to be a collection of outdated objects gradually reveals the human experiences and historical processes that helped shape later generations. By comparing the 2000s with life in 2050, the teenager begins to recognize that the present did not emerge from nowhere. It was shaped by the choices, creativity, struggles, and circumstances of those who came before.

Past in Pixels invites visitors to do more than revisit the past. It encourages them to question what they have inherited, recognize the influences shaping their own lives, and consider how their choices can contribute to the future. Although people cannot control every force around them, they can decide how to respond and how to participate in collective change.

Understanding what shaped us can help us decide what we want to shape next.

part2_storyline_preview.exe▫▫✕

Core Concept

Past in Pixels is a browser-based, interactive digital museum set within a story that begins in 2050. While helping prepare a family home for relocation, a teenager discovers a CRT monitor connected to Lola’s preserved memories. The monitor opens a digital museum that reconstructs Filipino popular culture from 2000 to 2010. Through personal memories, historical evidence, and interactive exploration, the teenager investigates how the past helped shape the world they now inhabit.

Story Progression

  1. The House We Leave BehindIn 2050, the family prepares to relocate because government redevelopment requires the area to be cleared or reconstructed. The house represents a place filled with family and community memories.
  2. The Forgotten RoomWhile sorting through belongings in their parent’s former room, the teenager discovers a CRT monitor and objects associated with Lola’s past. The family timeline and the exact history of these objects will be developed later.
  3. Entering the Digital MuseumThe teenager activates the monitor and discovers an interactive reconstruction of Filipino popular culture from 2000 to 2010.
  4. Exploring Lola’s WorldVisitors encounter experiences connected to communication, entertainment, identity, technology, globalization, and the other dimensions required by the project workbook. Historical sources and examples support the exhibits.
  5. Connecting the DecadesThe teenager compares the experiences of the 2000s with life in 2050, considering what changed, what persisted, and how historical processes influenced later generations.
  6. The Future We ShapeReturning to their own time, the teenager reflects on how understanding the past can inform present choices and collective responsibility.

Visitor Experience

The museum is planned as a web application visitors can access through a browser, with two playthrough options:

Standard · ~15 minFollows the essential storyline.
Extended · ~1 hourAdditional artifacts, historical evidence, comparisons, and reflection activities.

Both modes communicate the same central message, while the extended mode gives visitors more opportunities to investigate.

Intended Learning Outcomes

Recognize historical change and continuity in Filipino popular culture between 2000–2010 and more recent life.
Examine how social, cultural, economic, political, and technological circumstances shape people’s experiences and choices.
Reflect on personal and collective agency: although people cannot control every circumstance, they can make informed choices and contribute to change.

The family timeline, the precise history of the CRT monitor, the mechanism through which the teenager explores the museum, and the specific interactions remain open for further development. The current storyline is a foundation rather than a finalized script.

part3_ms2_group_reflection.exe▫▫✕

This reflection describes our group’s research, planning, progress, challenges, and next steps as we develop Past in Pixels into an evidence-based and engaging digital museum.

1. Application of Past and Present Materials

We will use the research gathered through Worksheets 2.1, 2.2, and 2.3 as the foundation of our digital museum, connecting Philippine popular culture from 2000–2010 with developments from 2020–2026. Rather than presenting the two periods as separate collections of facts, we aim to organize our findings into exhibits that demonstrate both cultural change and continuity.

For example, we can compare SMS and internet cafés with modern messaging platforms and digital communication; earlier television programs and music trends with online creators, streaming platforms, and contemporary Filipino music; and traditional remittance practices with digital payment services such as GCash and Maya. These comparisons will help visitors examine how communication, entertainment, work, and everyday life have evolved alongside technological and social changes. We will support the connections with relevant historical examples, statistics, and properly cited sources.

These materials will also support our storyline. Through the perspective of a teenager exploring Lola’s experiences in the 2000s, visitors will encounter historical developments through relatable situations rather than simply reading isolated facts. We want them to recognize continuities such as creativity, humor, belonging, family connections, and the desire for a better future.

Ultimately, we want the digital museum to show that Philippine popular culture is more than a collection of past trends. It reflects how people respond to changing circumstances, adapt technologies, express their identities, and influence the world around them.

2. Key Learnings, Achievements, and Challenges

Learnings

Understanding Philippine popular culture requires more than identifying entertainment trends or technological developments. We also need to examine the social, economic, political, and cultural circumstances that influence how people live, communicate, work, and express themselves. Although technologies and lifestyles change, some human experiences and cultural practices can persist across generations.

Achievements

We gathered research and developed comparisons through Worksheets 2.1, 2.2, and 2.3. We also established an initial creative direction and developed the concept for Past in Pixels, which connects historical exploration with a personal narrative.

Challenges

We have faced challenges in organizing a large amount of information while keeping explanations clear, accurate, and concise. Some areas still require stronger statistical evidence, more specific examples, and clearer connections between historical developments and their contemporary counterparts. We also need to translate written research into meaningful interactions.

Group coordination has also been a challenge. Although we established a timeline and assigned tasks, participation and follow-through were not always consistent, and delays reduced the time available for subsequent work. Planning alone is not enough; clear communication, accountability, and regular progress checks are necessary.

4. Current Project Status

We have established a research foundation through Worksheets 2.1, 2.2, and 2.3, covering communication technologies, entertainment, ICT, BPO, overseas migration, digital payment systems, and everyday life. We have identified connections between the two periods and developed an initial creative direction.

Our storyline is still being refined. We still need to finalize the narrative structure, organize historical material into exhibits, prepare visuals and references, select a web-development tool, and determine which interactions are feasible within our time and resources.

Our earlier estimate placed progress at approximately 65–70 percent, but this remains a provisional group-work estimate rather than a verified completion rate. Moving forward, we will prioritize unfinished deliverables, follow the assessment requirements closely, set internal deadlines, and communicate consistently.

As a group, we aim to create more than a digital collection of historical information. We want Past in Pixels to help visitors understand the people and experiences behind cultural change, recognize the connections between earlier generations and their own lives, and reflect on the choices available to them in a changing world. By strengthening our research, narrative, design, and collaboration, we hope to create an experience that preserves cultural memory while encouraging visitors to think critically about the future they will help shape.
